# Application of attack potential to FIDO L1+ Authenticator

Version 1.5.1 · Final Document · 2025-10-16 · **latest published** · [source](https://fidoalliance.org/specs/fido-security-requirements/FIDO-L1%2B-Application-of-Attack-Potential-v1.5.1-fd-20251016.html)

## Summary

The rating method labs use when evaluating L1+ authenticators, which are software authenticators protected by software techniques such as code obfuscation and white-box cryptography. It adapts ISO/IEC 18045 Annex B, the smartcard attack-potential method and Annex A of the TEE PP to software. Factors are elapsed time, expertise, knowledge of the TOE, window of opportunity, equipment and replicability, each scored separately for the identification and exploitation phases. Only complete attack paths are rated; partial attacks are steps within them. A summed score of 26 or more (Moderate) meets L1+, and 12 or more (Basic) meets L1. §7 gives examples of partial attacks on code and white-box crypto protections, and full attacks by local and remote expert attackers. Worked rating examples are in a separate attack-methods document. SR §2.2 points here, since L1+ has no companion program.

Status: latest published edition (v1.5.1, Final, 2025-10-16). Its alignment with SR v1.6/v1.7 numbering was not verified.

## Reading guide

**Start here:**
- §3 `#h2_scope`: the method rates only complete attack paths, and the listed attacks are a minimum set.
- §5.1 `#h3_determining-attack-potential`: separate identification and exploitation scores, and the target that L1+ must resist at least Moderate.
- §6 `#CalculationOfAttackPotential`: the score table and thresholds.

**Section by section:**
- §2 `#h2_introduction`: adapts ISO/IEC 18045 Annex B, the JIL smartcard method and TEE PP Annex A to software.
- §4.2 `#h3_definitions`: a partial attack shows a weakness in one mechanism. A full attack combines partial attacks into a practical compromise of an asset.
- §5.2 `#h3_factors-to-be-considered`, the factors:
  - Elapsed time `#h4_elapsed-time`: bands from ≤1 hour to >1 month; evaluators rarely spend more than 45 days.
  - Expertise `#h4_expertise`: layman to multiple experts. "Multiple" requires genuinely different skills, such as reverse engineering plus WBC cryptanalysis.
  - Knowledge of the TOE `#h4_knowledge-of-toe`: public / restricted (NDA) / sensitive / critical.
  - Window of opportunity `#h4_windows-of-opportunity`: redefined for software as how hard it is to get valid instances or enrolments without being noticed, since sample count does not matter.
  - Equipment `#h4_equipment`: emulators, disassemblers, DBI tools, public exploits and basic side-channel tools are Standard; DAST and advanced side-channel suites are Specialized.
  - Replicability `#replicability`, a new factor: Easy (generic, works on current devices), Moderate (needs root/jailbreak or old devices), Difficult (one specific device/OS and customised setup). It measures how easily the attack repeats, not its impact.
- §6 `#CalculationOfAttackPotential`: identification and exploitation values are summed. Replicability scores only in exploitation (0/3/6). Ratings: 0–11 no rating, 12–25 Basic (enough for L1), 26–32 Moderate (needed for L1+), >32 High.
- §7 `#h2_examples-of-software-attack-methods`:
  - Labs may ask for a test build with protections disabled. Any partial-attack rating made on it must show this in the window and knowledge factors, and must not report a total.
  - The TOE is the app-store binary.
  - Code protections (§7.1 `#h3_partial-attacks-on-the-code-protections`): reverse engineering and tracing.
  - WBC (§7.2 `#h3_partial-attacks-on-the-crypto-protections-wbc`): DCA and software-induced DFA only.
  - Full-attack scenarios: ECDSA key extraction by DCA followed by cloning, either with local device access (§7.3.1 `#h4_local-expert-attacker`) or through remote malware (§7.3.2 `#h4_remote-expert-attacker`). The remote case differs mainly in replicability.

**What matters most:**
- A total score belongs to a full attack path only, never to a partial attack. `#h2_scope` `#h2_examples-of-software-attack-methods`
- Vendors gain points mostly through device binding and anti-cloning (replicability) and through enrolment hurdles (window of opportunity). `#replicability` `#h4_windows-of-opportunity`

**Watch out for:**
- The body says DV 1.0.0, although the catalog version is 1.5.1 (§1.1 `#h3_version`).
- §5.2.4 defines a "None" window of opportunity, but the §6 table gives no value for it.
- Worked rating examples are in a FIDO-L1+-AM document that the references section does not list.
- SR §2.2.1 sets a 40 man-day / 10-week budget, while this document speaks of 45 days.

**Related documents:**
- `fido-authenticator-security-requirements`: §2.1.1 and §2.2.1 for the L1+ threat model and pen-test scope.
- `fido-authenticator-certification-policy`: §9.3.1.1 repeats this table for vulnerability rating; §9.5.2.2 gives the L1+ deadlines.
