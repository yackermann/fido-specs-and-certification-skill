# FIDO Authenticator Allowed Cryptography List

Version 1.7 · Review Draft · 2026-05-06 · **ACTIVE** · [source](https://fidoalliance.org/specs/fido-security-requirements/fido-authenticator-allowed-cryptography-list-v1.7-rd-20260506.html)

## Summary

Companion list to the Security and Privacy Requirements. It names the cryptographic algorithms FIDO accepts inside a certified authenticator and gives each one the security strength used to compute the authenticator's Overall Claimed Security Strength. It does not change which algorithms the FIDO protocols allow. §3 is organised by function: confidentiality, hashing, data authentication, key protection, random number generation, key agreement, key derivation, signatures and anonymous attestation. The RNG section sets requirements for TRNG/NRBG and DRBG designs and accepts RNGs validated under Common Criteria, FIPS 140-2 or FIPS 140-3. Post-quantum entries are included: ML-KEM-512/768/1024 for key agreement and ML-DSA-44/65/87 for signatures, with NIST quantum security categories explained in §3.1. §2 sets the conditions for proposing a new algorithm: at least 112-bit strength, non-proprietary, endorsed by a recognised standards body, and publicly reviewed.

Status: ACTIVE, although the file is labelled Review Draft (2026-05-06). v1.6 is active until 2027-01-13, and v1.5.1 goes with the L3/L3+ track.

**Who needs it:** authenticator vendors filling in the VQ crypto answers, FIDO-accredited security labs

## Reading guide

**Start here:**
- Abstract `#h2_abstract`: sets the scope. The list does not decide what a FIDO protocol allows. It limits the "internal" algorithms an authenticator picks for itself, and it supplies the strength value for every algorithm the authenticator uses.
- §3 intro `#h2_allowed-cryptographic-functions`: what a "Security Level (bits)" value means. It is a classical (not quantum) estimate against an attacker limited to 2^80 bytes of storage. A level of n means the best attack costs at least 2^(n-1) and less than 2^n operations.
- Then the table for the function you care about (§3.2–§3.10), and §2 `#h2_requirements-for-additional-candidates` if your algorithm is missing.

**Section by section:**
- §2 Requirements for Additional Candidates `#h2_requirements-for-additional-candidates`: to add a function, the vendor or lab writes an argument that it offers at least 112-bit strength, is not proprietary, gives the security properties its use needs, has a strength that can be characterised, is endorsed by ISO/IETF or a national/European body (NIST, ANSSI, BSI, SOGIS), and has had broad public review. A note warns the 112-bit floor may rise as NIST moves to PQC (IR 8547, SP 800-131A r3 draft).
- §3.1 Post-quantum cryptography `#h3_post-quantum-cryptography`: background only, no requirements. Gives the NIST category table (1–5, pegged to AES-128 … AES-256 and SHA3-256/384 collisions), notes FIPS 203/204 are published and HQC is next, and discusses hybrids. It explains why AES-128/192 and SHA-256 stay on the list (Grover gives little advantage).
- §3.2 Confidentiality `#h3_confidentiality-algorithms`: AES-128/192/256 at 128/192/256 bits.
- §3.3 Hashing `#h3_hashing-algorithms`: SHA-256/384/512 and SHA3-256/384/512 at half the digest length; SHA-512/t at t/2 for 256 ≤ t < 512. Includes an informative note that NSA removed SHA3 from CNSA 2.0.
- §3.4 Data Authentication `#h3_data-authentication-algorithms`: HMAC, CMAC and GMAC, with strength formulas (HMAC: the smallest of hash output length, half the hash state, and key length; CMAC/GMAC: cipher strength capped by tag length). Truncated tags should be at least 96 bits, and the level never exceeds the tag length. The primitives underneath must be on this list and at least 112-bit.
- §3.5 Key Protection `#h3_key-protection-algorithms`: the constructions used to wrap keys. These are SP 800-38F key wrap, GCM (IV of 96 bits or more, one fixed IV length per key), RSA-OAEP (keys generated per FIPS 186-5; 112 bits at 2048, 128 at 3072), CCM, Encrypt-then-HMAC and Encrypt-then-CMAC. The footnotes set conditions for the last two: independent keys, the MAC covers any IV/nonce/counter and, for HMAC with variable message sizes, the length goes in front of the variable-length parts.
- §3.6 Random Number Generator `#h3_random-number-generator`: an allowed RNG shall meet one of the two subsections. Evidence is either a certified platform RNG (Common Criteria, FIPS 140-2/140-3 or equivalent) or an evaluation by a FIDO-approved lab. The notes cover the FIPS 140-2 sunset (2026-09-21), seeding in line with SP 800-90B (IG 7.18 / FIPS 140-3 IG D.J), and RFC 6979-style hedging of (EC)DSA nonces through DRBG additional input.
  - TRNG/NRBG `#physical-true-trng-non-deterministic-random-number-bit-generator-nrbg-requirements`: AIS 20/31 PTG.2 or PTG.3 (PTG.2 may need post-processing; a CC Security Target with FCS_RNG.1 at those classes counts), SP 800-90B, or FIPS 140 validation with entropy source health tests. It counts as physical only if at least as much entropy goes in as comes out.
  - DRNG/DRBG `#deterministic-random-number-drng-bit-generator-drbg-requirements`: AIS 20/31 DRG.3/DRG.4 with seed entropy of at least the target level, SP 800-90A HMAC_DRBG / CTR_DRBG / Hash_DRBG at their instantiated strength, or FIPS 140 validation.
- §3.7 Key Agreement `#h3_key-agreement-algorithms`: DH-2048 (≥112), ECDH on P-256 or BrainpoolP256r1 (≥128), ML-KEM-512/768/1024 (NIST category 1/3/5).
- §3.8 KDFs `#h3_key-derivation-functions-kdfs`: SP 800-108 counter, feedback and double-pipeline modes, plus HKDF. Strength is the smaller of the key-derivation-key length and the PRF/HMAC strength, and the key-derivation key must be at least 112 bits. The CTAP PIN protocol 1 KDF is explicitly accepted even though it departs from SP 800-56C.
- §3.9 Signatures `#h3_signature-algorithms`: ECDSA P-256/384/521 (128/192/256), BrainpoolP256r1 (128), secp256k1 (126), RSA PSS and PKCS#1 v1.5 at 2048–4096 bits (all 112), SM2 with SM3 (128), Ed25519 (128), Ed448 (224), ML-DSA-44/65/87 (category 2/3/5). A note records NIST's end-2023 cut-off for PKCS#1 v1.5 and SOGIS legacy use until end 2030.
- §3.10 Anonymous Attestation `#h3_anonymous-attestation-algorithms`: ECDAA curves ED256-2 (112), ED512 (128) and ED638 (128). Strength is the smallest of hash strength, curve discrete log and embedding-field discrete log. The embedding field is usually what limits it.

**What matters most:**
- The numbers here cap your claims. In fido-authenticator-security-requirements (req 2.1.3), a key's claimed strength may not exceed its algorithm's value here and is zero if the algorithm is not listed. Req 2.1.4 then holds the Overall Claimed Security Strength to no more than any key's strength. One RSA-2048, DH-2048 or ED256-2 key therefore pins you at 112 (§3.7, §3.9, §3.10).
- Composite constructions are only as good as their parts. MACs, key wraps and KDFs must sit on listed primitives of at least 112 bits (§3.4 `#h3_data-authentication-algorithms`, §3.5 `#h3_key-protection-algorithms`, §3.8 `#h3_key-derivation-functions-kdfs`).
- The Encrypt-then-MAC and GCM conditions in §3.5 `#h3_key-protection-algorithms` (independent keys, IV under the MAC, fixed IV length) are easy to miss when you design your own key-wrapping or credential-wrapping scheme.
- RNG evidence (§3.6 `#h3_random-number-generator`) is a frequent gap. You need either a certificate for the platform RNG or a lab evaluation, and DRBG seeding has to meet SP 800-90B. Plan for FIPS 140-2 certificates going historical.
- PQC entries (§3.7, §3.9) give NIST categories, not bit values. The document does not say how a category turns into a bit value for the strength calculation, so agree on this with your lab.

**Watch out for:**
- The file is a Review Draft, and its boilerplate says not to implement from it, yet the catalog lists it as ACTIVE. v1.6 Final has the same tables and stays active until 2027-01-13.
- §3.1 is informative and partly dated (for example, an ANSSI publication announced for summer 2023).
- The note opening §3.9 limits "this curve" under FIPS 140 to blockchain use without naming the curve. The FIPS 186-5 / SP 800-186 context points to secp256k1.
- The §3.3 note says "SHA3-542" where SHA3-512 is meant. It is informative, and SHA3 stays allowed.
- The reference list has some mislabelled entries. [ISOIEC-10118-3] carries an SM3 title, [ISOIEC-14888-3] an SM2 title, and [ECDSA-ANSI] points to ANSI X9.63.
