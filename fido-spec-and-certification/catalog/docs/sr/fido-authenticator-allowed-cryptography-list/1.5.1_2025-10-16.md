# FIDO Authenticator Allowed Cryptography List

Version 1.5.1 · Final Document · 2025-10-16 · **ACTIVE, no expiry (L3/L3+)** · [source](https://fidoalliance.org/specs/fido-security-requirements/fido-authenticator-allowed-cryptography-list-v1.5.1-fd-20251016.html)

## Summary

v1.5.1 (Final, 2025-10-16) of the Allowed Cryptography List. It belongs to the v1.5.1 CTAP 2.1 / L3/L3+ track and keeps the pre-1.6 algorithm content. It has no ML-KEM or ML-DSA entries, still cites FIPS 186-4 and SP 800-108, and describes PQC as not yet standardised. RNGs validated under Common Criteria or FIPS 140-3 are accepted. §2 frames new-algorithm requests in terms of internal use or claiming a non-zero strength. Status: ACTIVE with no expiry, together with SR v1.5.1 for L3/L3+.

**Who needs it:** vendors certifying CTAP 2.1 authenticators against SR v1.5.1 (L3/L3+), FIDO-accredited security labs

**Look here for:**
- Allowed algorithms and claimed strengths → §3 `#h2_allowed-cryptographic-functions`
- Signature algorithms → §3.9 `#h3_signature-algorithms`
- RNG requirements → §3.6 `#h3_random-number-generator`
- Conditions for adding an algorithm → §2 `#h2_requirements-for-additional-candidates`

## Reading guide

**Use this version when:** you certify a CTAP 2.1 authenticator on the SR v1.5.1 track, in particular L3/L3+, where this edition stays active with no expiry. Despite its October 2025 date, the content follows the pre-1.6 baseline. Do not assume it contains what v1.6/v1.7 added.

**Differences from v1.6/v1.7:**
- No post-quantum entries. §3.7 `#h3_key-agreement-algorithms` lists only DH-2048 and ECDH (P-256, BrainpoolP256r1), and §3.9 `#h3_signature-algorithms` has no ML-DSA. §3.1 `#h3_post-quantum-cryptography` still describes the NIST round-3 candidates, and the §3 intro says no quantum-safe asymmetric standard exists yet.
- §2 `#h2_requirements-for-additional-candidates` asks for the written argument whenever a vendor wants to use an unlisted function internally where an Allowed algorithm is required, or to claim a non-zero strength for it. Later versions frame the same criteria as adding a function to the list.
- §3.6 `#h3_random-number-generator`: platform evidence names Common Criteria or FIPS 140-3 only, and the transition notes date from 2019–2021.
  - §3.6.1 `#h4_physical-true-trng-non-deterministic-random-number-bit-generator-nrbg-requirements` accepts AIS 20/31 PTG.2/PTG.3, an SP 800-90C NRBG (any strength, with a live entropy source) or a FIPS 140-2 validation with entropy health tests. It notes that DRBGs in FIPS modules rarely qualify.
  - §3.6.2 `#h4_deterministic-random-number-drng-bit-generator-drbg-requirements` accepts AIS 20/31 DRG.3/DRG.4, SP 800-90A DRBGs or FIPS 140-3 validation, and says seeding must move to full SP 800-90B compliance.
- §3.10 `#h3_anonymous-attestation-algorithms` keeps the rule that maps embedding-field size to strength: 3072–7679 bits counts as 128 and 2048–3071 bits as 112.
- References are older: FIPS 186-4 (for RSA-OAEP key generation and the signature tables), SP 800-108 (2009) and SP 800-56C r1. SOGIS legacy use of PKCS#1 v1.5 ends in 2022 here.
- The classical algorithm tables (AES, SHA-2/SHA-3, HMAC/CMAC/GMAC, key wrapping, KDFs, ECDSA/RSA/SM2/EdDSA, ECDAA) carry the same strength values as v1.6/v1.7.

**Section by section:** otherwise as in v1.7; see that page's reading guide. The RNG subsections here are numbered §3.6.1/§3.6.2 and use `h4_` anchors.
